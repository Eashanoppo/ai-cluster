# 24. Capacity Management

## 24.1 Purpose

The **Capacity Management Engine** is the **Resource Planning and Allocation Layer** of the AI Cloud Operating System (AIOS).

Its purpose is to continuously monitor, predict, optimize, and manage the capacity of the entire AI infrastructure to ensure that workloads can be executed efficiently while maintaining SLA compliance, minimizing operational costs, and maximizing hardware utilization.

Unlike traditional systems that react only after resources become exhausted, AIOS proactively forecasts future demand and automatically prepares the cluster before bottlenecks occur.

The Capacity Management Engine works closely with:

* processors.py
* Scheduling Engine
* Cost Optimization Engine
* Digital Twin Engine
* Failure Prediction Engine
* Kubernetes
* Ray
* Prometheus
* NVIDIA DCGM

---

The Capacity Management Engine shall:

# 24.6 Capacity States

The entire cluster operates under five capacity states.

| Capacity  | Status                  | AIOS Action                                                                                                  |
| --------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 0–60%     | Healthy                 | Normal autonomous scheduling                                                                                 |
| 60–75%    | Optimize                | Consolidate resources and optimize placement                                                                 |
| 75–90%    | Warning                 | Notify operators, prepare autoscaling, optimize aggressively                                                 |
| 90–95%    | Critical                | Queue lower-priority workloads, scale infrastructure, reserve capacity                                       |
| Above 95% | Human Approval Required | processors.py requests operator approval before terminating active workloads or executing disruptive actions |

These thresholds are configurable through system policies.

# 24.10 Autoscaling Integration

Capacity Management coordinates Kubernetes autoscaling.

Scale-Out Triggers

* GPU utilization exceeds threshold.
* Queue growth continues.
* SLA risk increases.
* Capacity forecast predicts shortages.

Scale-In Triggers

* Long-term idle resources.
* Low utilization.
* Successful workload consolidation.
* Reduced demand forecasts.

Scaling decisions are executed by processors.py.

# 24.21 Functional Requirements

The Capacity Management Engine shall:

* Continuously monitor GPU, CPU, memory, storage, and network capacity across the AIOS infrastructure.
* Predict future demand using historical telemetry, workload trends, and simulator scenarios.
* Coordinate with Kubernetes and Ray to support proactive autoscaling and intelligent resource allocation.
* Maintain configurable capacity states, thresholds, quotas, and resource reservations.
* Prioritize workloads according to SLA, business policies, and workload priority.
* Validate major capacity decisions using the Digital Twin before production execution whenever feasible.
* Integrate with the Scheduling, Cost Optimization, Failure Prediction, and Learning Engines through `processors.py`.
* Record all capacity decisions, forecasts, and outcomes for auditing and continuous improvement.
* Provide real-time dashboards, alerts, and explainable capacity decisions through Grafana and the Next.js frontend.
* Operate autonomously while requesting human approval only when policy-defined thresholds require manual intervention or when disruptive actions affecting active workloads are necessary.
